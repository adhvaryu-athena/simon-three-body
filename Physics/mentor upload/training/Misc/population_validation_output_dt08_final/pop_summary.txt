To confirm that SIMON's generalisation is not limited to the seven
hand-selected initial conditions in Section~\ref{sec:multi_ic},
we evaluated both SIMON and \texttt{ias15} on 500 randomly
generated three-body configurations with masses sampled log-uniformly
from [0.50--2.0], [0.005--0.50], and
[0.001--0.10]~M_{\odot} (all within SIMON's training
distribution) and separations drawn from a non-crossing hierarchical
population filtered to avoid persistent close encounters below the
validated SIMON operating regime.
SIMON and \texttt{ias15} agreed on the bounded/ejected classification
for 96.0% of configurations (\texttt{ias15} ejection
rate 22.4%; SIMON ejection rate 24.8%).
For the 372 bounded configurations, the mean divergence
rate was \lambda = 0.023 \pm 0.021~yr$^{-1}$,
consistent with the seven-IC result of
$\lambda = 0.092 \pm 0.068$~yr$^{-1}$,
confirming that the hand-selected configurations are representative
of the broader population. The mean per-IC speedup over \texttt{ias15}
was 0.28\times~(median 0.26\times).
