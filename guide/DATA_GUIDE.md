## Finding 1 — Time-reversal symmetry governs energy drift

> **Correction — pure-Newton re-run** (`abstract/soft_vs_newton_diff.txt`). Finding 1 was originally computed in softened mode. Re-run in pure Newtonian (the advocated method): the IC1 fixed-step dt=0.005 “128% ejection” is a softening artifact — in pure Newton that run is bounded at 0.19% (switching discontinuity in the softened force at the 0.15 AU / 5×10⁻⁴ boundaries, not the dynamics). tsalf is mode-independent (IC1 0.087 / IC4 0.224 / IC6 0.003%); IC3, IC6 and wide binaries are bit-identical soft↔newton. Findings 2 and 3 unaffected.

### Claim: efficiency advantage is regime-dependent (not a universal speedup)

> ⚠️ The per-regime multipliers below are **softened-mode** and partly rest on the IC1 "ejection
> band" artifact (see the pure-Newton correction note above). The direction of the claim holds
> (regime-dependent; no universal factor), which is why the published abstract/outline keep only
> the **qualitative** "lower cost on close-encounter/under-resolved systems" statement and drop the
> specific multiplier. Exact pure-Newton multipliers are deferred to the full (Aug) paper.

**Source:** `abstract/speed_table.txt`

Force evaluations to reach equal accuracy on the energy metric, tsalf vs fixed leapfrog:

| IC | Regime | tsalf cheaper by | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| IC1 | Close encounter | **1.1×–3.6×** | leapfrog has ejection band; only reliable at very fine dt |
| IC4 | Under-resolved | **1.5×–2.9×** | tsalf monotone; leapfrog monotone but starts from high error |
| IC3 | Near-regular | 0.32×–0.51× (**slower**) | fixed leapfrog already cheap |
| IC6 | Near-regular | 0.74×–1.53× (mixed) | fixed leapfrog competitive |

Full ratio range across all ICs and accuracy targets: **0.32× to 3.57×** (regime-dependent).  
There is no single universal speedup number.

## Finding 3 — Neural network corrections are null at equal compute

> 🔧 **Code-correction note (nn_correctors, June 2026).** Seven implementation issues were fixed in
> `experiments/nn_correctors/` (A2 residual sign; A2 window timing; A1 start half-kick c; `bounded`
> = physical not just finite; `pair_fidelity` min+max; config-count assertions; IAS15-skip logging).
> See `experiments/nn_correctors/FIXES.md`. A corrected-inference diagnostic on the **existing**
> weights confirms the **verdict is unchanged: A1 and A2 remain NULL** (neither beats an equal-compute
> finer step). The per-config win counts below will shift slightly once the canonical artifacts are
> regenerated on the rig (`run_corrected.sh`); the table below is the pre-fix run and will be updated
> after that retrain. The oracle result (next subsection) is unaffected.

### Two correctors were evaluated

| Label | Type | When it fires | Training data |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Per-step scalar force-magnitude correction | Every leapfrog step | IAS15-matched close-encounter dataset |
| **A2** | Encounter-triggered state-to-state residual | Only at close approach (~1×/100 yr on IC1) | Same dataset |

### Equal-compute results (27 held-out configurations)

**Source:** `abstract/nn_evaluation_table.txt` and `experiments/nn_correctors/results_table.txt`

Protocol: corrector "clicks" only if it beats the plain-leapfrog cost–accuracy frontier at the
same total compute (NN is charged κ floating-point operations per force evaluation).

| Corrector | Metric | κ=0 (NN free) | κ=10 | κ=90 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **short-horizon RMS** | **0/27** | **0/27** | **0/27** | NULL |
| A1 | energy max\|dE\| | 8/27 | 4/27 | 1/27 | NULL (energy wins vanish with cost) |
| A2 | **short-horizon RMS** | **2/27** | **1/27** | **1/27** | NULL |
| A2 | energy max\|dE\| | 5/27 | 3/27 | 1/27 | NULL |

Short-horizon RMS is the honest chaos-era metric (energy is gameable by the NN).
A1 wins 0/27 even when given free compute — this is the headline null.

**Full per-config breakdown:** `experiments/nn_correctors/results_table.txt`  
**Plot:** `abstract/nn_vs_compute.png` (Figure 3 — wins-of-27 vs κ)

### Oracle result: failure is temporal resolution, not force accuracy

**Source:** `abstract/nn_evaluation_table.txt` (binary-single oracle section)

A perfect oracle correction (best possible force correction at every close encounter) yields:

| Binary separation r_bin | Oracle improvement | Gate fires |
|---|---|---|
| 0.01 AU | **0.0%** | 0.0% |
| 0.05 AU | **0.0%** | 0.0% |
| 0.10 AU | **0.0%** | 0.0% |
| 0.20 AU | **0.0%** | 0.0% |
| 0.40 AU | **0.0%** | 0.0% |

The gate never fires because the binary is under-resolved at the step level — no encounter
correction can fix a timestep problem. tsalf resolves it correctly at the same separations.

## The operating envelope (synthesis)

**Source:** `abstract/frontier_table.txt` (last section)

| Regime | Sufficient integrator | Example config | Cost (fe/yr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular / well-separated | Fixed leapfrog dt=0.04 | IC6: 0.0006% | 25 |
| Moderate scattering | Time-symmetric adaptive η=0.05 | IC1: 0.087%, IC4: 0.224% | 75–88 |
| Tight binary (r_bin ≤ 0.05 AU) | IAS15 | BS_0.05: bounded; tsalf needs 41,241 fe/yr vs IAS15's 31,593 | ~31,600 |

For tight binaries, both IAS15 and tsalf are bounded, but IAS15 is faster (both in force
evaluations and wall-clock). The heuristic and fixed leapfrog both eject at all tested separations.

**Population sweep (400 configurations):** `experiments/track5_population/track5_results.txt`  
tsalf bounded 400/400 (100%) vs leapfrog 394/400; on close-encounter configs 10/10 vs 6/10.

## What NOT to claim (internal — do not include in paper)

**Source:** `experiments/track1_reconciliation/reconciliation.txt`

The original paper's "Run A → 161.7 AU ejection" is **not reproduced at matched dt=0.04**.
At the operational dt, Run A (no adaptive) is bounded at 0.684% — the *lowest* drift of the
three ablation runs. The ejection only appears at finer dt (dt=0.02: 417%, ejected;
dt=0.01: 9253%, ejected), which is a known non-monotonicity of leapfrog on IC1, not evidence
that adaptive stepping is necessary at the operational setting.

→ Do not claim the adaptive mechanism prevents ejection at dt=0.04. It does not.
